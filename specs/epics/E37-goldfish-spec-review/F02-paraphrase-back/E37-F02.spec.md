# Doc-critic paraphrase-back comprehension mode at the feature-spec checkpoint — Functional Spec

> R-ids in this document are `R1 … R8`; their fully-qualified form is `E37-F02:R1 … E37-F02:R8`.

## Context

The Doc-critic at `target-type=feature-spec` is told to evaluate five dimensions and flag
only downstream-breaking issues. Nothing makes it prove it understood the spec, and the
author cannot test that on itself: the Architect shares the spec's blind spots, and the
Builder — who reads the four files cold, in a clean context, with no inbox brief and no chat
— is the first reader who finds out whether they carry their meaning on their own. By then a
misreading costs a build → review → PR round (the epic's evidence: the E36-F04/E36-F06 re-spec
round, the E99-F07 assertion that recurred three times). EGM's Comprehension Goldfish closes
this with a method, not a persona: the cold reader first restates "what this proposes" and
"how the existing system works, per this document", and anything it cannot restate is a
Blocker. This feature ships that method as a **mode inside the one portable Doc-critic role
file** — no second persona, no synthesizer, no fan-out — with the bound the brief chose:
**one** re-audit, then leftovers become `## Accepted trade-offs` in the `.spec.md` so the
decision is durable and visible to the Reviewer and to the human at the gate.

This is **prose** — a change to how a model is instructed, not a tool — so `docs/RATIONALE.md`
classifies it as *capability compensation* with an ablation threshold (ledger row `C12`,
principle 1 of the ablation doctrine: keep it only while its absence reproduces a spec defect
reaching review). The users are the Architect (gets the paraphrase and the blocking findings
inline before `spec-ready`), the Builder (receives a spec a cold reader could restate), the
Reviewer (reads the recorded trade-offs instead of re-litigating them) and the human at the
gate (sees what was knowingly left unresolved).

**The brief's two open questions, resolved.**

1. *Hard block or a flag the human sees?* **Fix-or-record, bounded by one re-audit** — the
   brief's lean, mirroring EGM. The harness's gate vocabulary is closed
   (`tests/test_doc_critic.sh` R17/R18 pin the five-value status enum and the role's
   `never block` sentence; `docs/WORKFLOW.md` states the critic "never blocks or introduces a
   human gate"), so "blocking" is realised exactly as the readiness lint's "must exit 0" is:
   a **precondition of the Architect's own hand-off**, written in `agents/architect.md`, not a
   power of the critic. A `could-not-restate` finding obliges the Architect to fix the unit or
   record it under `## Accepted trade-offs` before hand-off; the critic itself stays advisory
   — no new status, no new gate, no schema change. The human at the gate sees the recorded
   trade-offs because they are in the spec it approves.
2. *Where is the checkpoint note persisted today?* `agents/doc-critic.md` → `## Output
   summary`: `progress/<run>/doc-critic-<checkpoint>.md` ("or a similarly scoped path"); the
   generated Claude shim names the same path, and in practice `<run>` is the feature id
   (`progress/E37-F02/doc-critic-feature-spec.md`). The brief's `progress/<feature>-doc-critic.md`
   is **not** the current path. The `## Paraphrase` section opens **that** note (and the
   critic's reply to the Architect carries the same text), so nothing about where notes live
   changes.

**What a paraphrase is, for calibration.** Restating in the critic's own words what the four
files say — quoting the spec's sentences back is not a paraphrase, and knowledge the critic
has from elsewhere (its training, other harness files) does not count: a gap it can fill only
from outside the document is a gap **in the document**. A unit counts as `could-not-restate`
only when its **own meaning** is missing from the four files — not because it cites a file,
test, lesson or check the critic cannot open; a cross-reference whose purpose the unit states
is restated (otherwise every harness-meta spec would yield one blocking item per citation, and
the Architect's easy exit would be to record them all as trade-offs). A unit the critic can
restate but thinks wrong is an ordinary five-dimension finding, not a `could-not-restate`.
The paraphrase is written before anything else the critic does at this checkpoint: the two
lints and the architecture/ADR consistency checks read other files and run after it. If the
critic errors or times out there is no list to act on — the Architect proceeds best-effort and
records the skip, exactly as today. The first live
exercise of the mode is the next feature's checkpoint (E37-F03): this feature's own
checkpoint ran under the pre-change role, so this spec records no trade-offs and the suite
pins the **text** of the contract — the behavior is what the following checkpoints measure,
and the `C12` ledger row names the evidence that would remove it.

Two items go beyond the inbox brief's in-scope list and are intentional, directed by the
Orchestrator's spec task for this feature: the one `README.md` sentence (the README names the
three spec lints beside which the mode sits) and the `docs/RATIONALE.md` row's exact
threshold wording. One rule is the Architect's own addition: a `## Accepted trade-offs` entry
that names no unit or gives no reason is a Reviewer **flag**, not a reject — without it a
malformed record would leave the Reviewer with no defined verdict.

**Complexity basis.** `standard`: 8 of 12 `R-id`s (not near the budget); no ADR whose
constraints reach across several files is touched (ADR-0008 governs only this spec's task
shape); no paid defect class — the generated shims (`.claude/agents/doc-critic.md` and its
siblings) embed no mode text, so there is no body-and-copy to diverge, and
`sh harness-install.sh --self` must show **no** diff; and speccing it resolved no
contradiction between two components — the "blocking yet advisory" tension is closed by
definition (an Architect-side obligation) without changing any component's contract.

## Business rules

- **One role file, one new section, at `feature-spec` only.** `agents/doc-critic.md` gains
  one section, headed exactly `## Paraphrase-back comprehension (E37-F02)`, directly after
  `## Review scope and calibration (what to flag)` and before `## Plan-row citations and
  stage-aware NFRs (E36-F04)` — so the E36-F04 section still sits between `## Review scope
  and calibration` and `## Advisory-only behavior` (`tests/test_plan_cite.sh` R9) and the
  E37-F01 section still sits between E36-F04 and `## Epic-decomposition checks`
  (`tests/test_readiness_check.sh` R9). The section applies at `target-type=feature-spec`
  only and says so; `plan-output` and `epic-decomposition` are unchanged (a later feature may
  extend them). No second persona, no CLI-specific wording (`tests/test_doc_critic.sh`
  portability row: the role never names Claude Code).
- **The paraphrase comes first and is written from the four files alone.** At `feature-spec`
  the critic's note opens with a `## Paraphrase` section — before the findings — written from
  `<feature>.spec.md`, `<feature>.plan.md`, `<feature>.tasks.md` and `<feature>.tests.md`
  **only**: the critic does not open the inbox brief under `progress/inbox/`, the epic's
  `epic.md`, the architecture or any other file while writing it, and reads no chat history
  (the Architect passes it nothing else). The reason is stated in the role: the Builder
  receives only these four files, so the paraphrase measures what they carry on their own.
- **Three parts, in order.** `### What this proposes` (the change and the behavior it adds, in
  the critic's own words); `### How the existing system works, per this document` (what the
  four files say about the components, contracts and tools the feature changes and what they
  do today — only what the document states or implies); `### Could not restate` — one list per
  file, each item naming the unit (an `R-id`, a `## Files to change` row by its first cell, a
  task by its `T<n>` id, a tests-table row by its `R-id`, or a section heading) and one line
  on what is missing or contradictory; `none` for a file whose every unit the critic could
  restate. A unit counts only when its own meaning is missing from the four files, not when it
  cites a file, test, lesson or check the critic cannot open. The lints and every other check of
  this checkpoint run **after** the paraphrase is written.
- **A `could-not-restate` item is a blocking finding — of the Architect's hand-off, not a
  gate.** Each `### Could not restate` item is repeated in the findings as
  `could-not-restate <file> <unit> — <what is missing>` (a stable finding id, the shape the
  epic-decomposition findings use). "Blocking" means the Architect must **fix the unit or
  record it under `## Accepted trade-offs` in the `.spec.md` before hand-off**, bounded by one
  re-audit; the critic still never blocks the Architect, there is no new status and no new
  human gate, every finding outside this list stays advisory, and the role's existing
  `## Advisory-only behavior` and `## Best-effort failure posture` sections stand unchanged.
  On a re-audit the critic writes the paraphrase again from the revised files and lists only
  what it still cannot restate.
- **The Architect's checkpoint step: paraphrase first, apply, at most ONE re-audit, record.**
  `agents/architect.md` → `## Doc-critic checkpoint before \`spec-ready\` (R12)` keeps its
  existing paragraph (spawn the Doc-critic with `target-type=feature-spec`, pass the four
  paths, apply advisory findings inline, best-effort on error — the anchors
  `tests/test_doc_critic.sh` R12 and `tests/test_source_shims.sh` read) and gains a paragraph
  that: passes the critic nothing but the four files (not the inbox brief, not the
  conversation); reads the paraphrase before the findings and treats a restatement that
  differs from what the Architect meant as a defect of the document; fixes each
  `could-not-restate` unit and spawns the critic once more on the revised files — **at most
  one re-audit per feature**; records every `could-not-restate` still listed after the re-audit
  — including one the fixes introduced — or that the Architect chooses not to fix, in the
  `.spec.md` under `## Accepted trade-offs` as
  `- <unit> — <what the cold reader could not restate> — accepted because <why>`; states that a
  leftover neither fixed nor recorded is not a finished checkpoint; skips the re-audit when
  the first pass lists no `could-not-restate`; and, when the critic errors or times out, has no
  list to act on — proceeds best-effort and records the skip as the existing paragraph says.
  The `## Hand-off` section's condition
  ("the doc-critic checkpoint has completed") is thereby defined; its text is untouched.
- **The Reviewer reads `## Accepted trade-offs`; a recorded trade-off is not a defect.**
  `agents/reviewer.md` gains a section headed exactly `## Accepted trade-offs (E37-F02)`
  directly after `## What you check` (after item 9, before `## ADR-citation check`): when the
  `.spec.md` carries the section, read it before judging the spec's prose; a recorded
  trade-off is a decision recorded by the Architect and visible at the human gate, **not a
  defect** — the Reviewer neither rejects nor flags the named unit for the comprehension gap it
  records and does not ask the Builder to resolve it. The record waives **only that**: the
  unit's `R-id` still needs a passing test (check 2), the three spec lints still decide on
  their exit codes (checks 7, 8, 9), an over-claiming sentence is still a defect (3c), a
  contradiction with a collaborator file is still check 5's reject. An entry naming no unit or
  giving no reason is a flag ("record the unit and the why"), not a reject. Items 1–9 of
  `## What you check` are unchanged (`tests/test_plan_cite.sh` and
  `tests/test_readiness_check.sh` slice items 2, 7, 8 and 9 by number).
- **The template carries the optional section.** `specs/_templates/feature.spec.md` gains
