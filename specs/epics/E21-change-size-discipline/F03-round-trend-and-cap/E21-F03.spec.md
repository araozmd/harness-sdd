# Per-round finding trend at the round cap — Functional Spec

## Context
`pr_loop.max_rounds` (default 4) caps the loop and labels the PR `needs-human`. What it does
not do is say **what the human should conclude**. In practice the conclusion drawn is "run it
again": on `viernes-bookings-api` PR #76 the operator posted `@codex review` twelve times by
hand. Rounds 5–12 cost roughly 2M input tokens and 8 hours of wall clock to keep rediscovering
that the diff was too large to review in one pass.

The information that would have ended it after round 4 was already on the PR, unaggregated —
the per-round blocking-finding count: `1 3 1 2 1 3 1 2 2 1 2 1`.

A **decaying** count means the review is converging and one more round is rational. A **flat**
count means the reviewer is sampling a surface larger than one pass can cover, so more rounds
buy more samples, not more confidence. Those two situations are indistinguishable at the cap
today.

## Business rules
- Advisory only: it never changes **when** the cap fires, never blocks, never merges.
- Reads only the loop's existing round cache. No new persisted state, no `gh`, no network —
  everything is rebuildable from `round-*/blocking.json`.
- The rule must be **explainable inside the `needs-human` message**. A least-squares slope
  would be defensible and unreadable; "the last 3 rounds each produced a blocking finding" is
  what a human can check by eye.
- A round that aborted before classifying has no `blocking.json` and therefore no count.
  Treating its absence as zero would fake a convergence that never happened.

## Architecture alignment
ADRs touched: none — additive reporting over an existing cache; no change to the loop's
exit-code contract, freshness guards, or clean-signal detection.

## Acceptance criteria (EARS)

- **R1** — When the last 3 rounds with a readable `blocking.json` each produced at least one
  blocking finding, the system shall report verdict `non-converging`.
- **R2** — When the finding count decays to zero in the most recent round, the system shall
  report verdict `converging`.
- **R3** — Where fewer than 3 rounds carry a readable `blocking.json`, the system shall report
  verdict `insufficient` rather than defaulting to either other verdict.
- **R4** — If a round directory exists without a readable `blocking.json`, then the system
  shall exclude it from the series rather than counting it as zero findings.
- **R5** — The system shall exit 0 at every verdict, and exit 4 only for a usage error
  (missing/unknown `--cache`, bad `--format`, bad `--window`).
- **R6** — While the verdict is `non-converging`, the system shall instruct the reader to
  **split the PR** and shall list the paths the blocking findings concentrate on; and while it
  is `converging` it shall not give that instruction.
- **R7** — The `/sdd-pr-loop` command shall run the trend each round and carry the verdict into
  the handover summary and, at the cap, into the `needs-human` message.
- **R8** — The **installed** `/sdd-pr-loop` body — the one the installer generates, not only
  the source-layout copy — shall carry the trend step, and `tools/pr-round-trend.sh` shall be
  installed executable.

## Out of scope
- Changing when the cap fires, or `max_rounds` / `blocking_severities` semantics.
- Auto-splitting anything — that needs a human decision (and E21-F04's lane).
- Auto-closing or auto-labelling beyond the existing `needs-human` label.

## Open questions
- Whether the trend should also fire *before* the cap (e.g. at round 3 of 4) as an early
  warning. It would be the difference between advice at hour 2 and advice at hour 4; deferred
  because the cap is the point where a human is already reading.
- Rounds are per-invocation today, but the trend is most meaningful across a PR's whole
  history. Re-running `/sdd-pr-loop` on the same PR reuses `.pr-loop/<pr>/`, so the series does
  span invocations — but nothing guarantees the round numbering stays monotonic if the cache
  is partially cleared.
