# Intent — the increment contract: delimitation, ownership, review scope

> **Read `progress/inbox/E21-F05.md` first.** That is the doctrine document and this depends on
> it. This brief carries the half of the old E21-F05 that **all three of Codex's P1 findings
> landed on**, and every role-file contradiction with it.

## ⚠️ Answer this before speccing: does the lane earn its keep?

**The sibling-feature model already delivers budget-sized reviews** — E21's actual goal — with
**zero new machinery**. `agents/driller.md:85-87` and `E21-F01` R4 already split an over-budget
candidate into sibling features sequenced by `depends_on`, and each sibling is its own board
feature with its own PR and its own review.

Stacking adds **exactly one thing** over that: **wall-clock overlap**. Under siblings,
`next()` gates F0b on F0a being `done` and the wait-for-merge rule holds it until F0a merges, so
every increment serialises a full review cycle. Under a stack, increment 1 is in review while
increment 3 is still being written.

**The corollary that makes this urgent, and it cuts the other way:** under the sibling model
*no PR ever targets a non-default base*, so `tools/pr-stack-guard.sh` never fires and
**E21-F04's merged mechanism has no caller** — the state F04 was withdrawn for once already.

So the question is narrow and it is a **product judgement, not an Architect's call**:

> Is avoiding that serialisation worth a delimiter convention, four role-file contracts, a
> manual restack burden, and a merge-order guard?

**If the answer is no, this feature is not built** and the honest follow-up is a small feature
that deprecates the lane in `docs/WORKFLOW.md` (which E21-F05 will already have made accurate)
and decides what becomes of `pr-stack-guard.sh`. **Do not spec this feature until that question
is answered.**

## Problem / who

E21-F04 makes `/sdd-pr-loop` *safe* against a non-default base. E21-F05 says what stacking is
and is not. Neither says **how an increment is delimited, who opens its PR, or how a partial
increment is reviewed** — and without those three the lane has no callers.

## The three findings this feature must close

All three verified against the cited files on 2026-08-12; none disputed.

1. **Who opens an increment's PR.** The withdrawn spec said the Builder. `agents/reviewer.md:34-38`
   uses *that exact contradiction* as its worked example — *"told the Builder to open a child
   repo's PR, contradicting `builder.md` Loop A's precondition that the **Builder never opens a
   PR** … load `builder.md`, quote Loop A, and hard reject."* The correction is free: the
   **Orchestrator** opens each increment PR after the Reviewer approves it, which is where it
   opens every other PR (`agents/orchestrator.md` → *The pre-PR change-size handoff*). No carried
   decision is touched — decision 1 is about the stack being *grown*, not about who runs
   `gh pr create`. Knock-on: `agents/builder.md`'s section then shows **zero** `gh pr create`.
2. **No path exists for the Reviewer to approve a non-final increment.**
   `agents/reviewer.md:11-13` rejects unless every `R-id` has a passing test; on increment 1 of 3
   most have none. And `agents/reviewer.md:213` says *"**Approve** → tell the Orchestrator to set
   `done`"* — the whole feature. Holding the `done` **write** in the Orchestrator is necessary and
   not sufficient: it never tells the Reviewer how to **scope verification** or what its verdict
   means mid-stack. `agents/reviewer.md` therefore **moves into scope**; excluding it was a gap,
   not a defensible boundary.
3. **`depends_on` cannot delimit increments inside one feature.** It sequences **sibling board
   features** (`agents/driller.md:85-87`, `E21-F01` R4), while the stack is deliberately one
   feature with no `increments[]`. So neither the feature's `depends_on` nor its flat task list
   says which tasks comprise increment N. **The candidate that keeps the lane alive is
   delimiting increments in `tasks.md`** — an `## Increment N — <name>` grouping, which the
   `done` trigger already half-assumes when it looks for an unticked task. It costs a third
   behaviour: the **Architect** must emit the groups, and `agents/architect.md` references
   `docs/WORKFLOW.md` **nowhere** (verified: zero occurrences), so a doctrine-only convention
   never reaches the agent that writes `tasks.md`.

## Success outcome

A Builder handed a large-but-safely-splittable feature can grow a reviewed stack, and every role
contract says the same thing about it: where increments are cut, who opens each PR, what the
Reviewer verifies and what its verdict means, and when `done` is written.

## Scope / boundaries

**In scope**
- The increment **delimiter** convention and the artifact that carries it.
- **PR ownership** and merge order; the manual restack procedure's *contract* half.
- **Reviewer scope and verdict semantics** for a non-final increment.
- The **`done` rollup trigger** — a signal derivable from existing artifacts, since the board
  records no "increment 2 of 3".
- `agents/architect.md`, `agents/builder.md`, `agents/orchestrator.md`, `agents/reviewer.md`.

**Out of scope**
- `docs/WORKFLOW.md`'s doctrine section — **E21-F05**, which must merge first.
- E21-F04's guard and merge gate.
- Automating the split of an existing branch; any third-party stacked-PR tool.

## Decisions

- **Carried, do not re-derive:** the stack is **grown**, not created up front; `done` rolls only
  when the **last** increment merges, with no per-increment board record.
- **Under pressure, and the gate's to settle — decision 3, "the board does not represent a
  stack".** Finding 3 exists *because* of it. Adding the deferred `increments[]` would make the
  delimiter, the review scope and the `done` trigger directly expressible, and would likely bring
  the R-id count back under budget. Its original reasons stand: it duplicates ~80% of `slices[]`
  across `store/tasks.schema.json`, `store/local.md`, both mirror adapters, `tools/sync-board.mjs`
  and `tools/next-task.mjs` — and selection is *code* now (**ADR-0001**), so it lands inside the
  harness's most bug-prone surface, which is the one that ADR exists to protect. **Reversing it is
  a re-drill, not a spec edit**: it changes the schema and therefore the epic's shape.

## Constraints

- **No atomicity language anywhere.**
- **Cross-file consistency is the whole risk.** Four role files, plus `.claude/agents/` shims.
  Verified: the shims are pointer stubs (`opencode.json` interpolates
  `{file:./.harness/agents/<role>.md}`), so the doctrine must be **cited, not copied** — and the
  citation asserted, not hoped for.
- A statement that must hold across files needs a **fence-aware** section extractor and must
  never bind a possibly-zero `grep -c` under `set -eu`. Both were shipped defects in the
  withdrawn spec; see `progress/E21-F05-doc-critic-feature-spec.md`.
- Do not freeze an exact `VERSION` string, and do not diff DO-NOT-TOUCH files against `main`.
- Must stay inert when `pr_loop.enabled: false`.
- Scoped to **~7 R-ids**. If it grows past 12, stop and report.

## Open questions

- **Where does the delimiter live** — an `## Increment N` grouping in `tasks.md`, or something
  else? Whatever is chosen must be assertable and must not require a schema change.
- **What does a Reviewer verdict on a non-final increment mean?** "Ready for its PR" is the
  candidate. Name it, and say what the Orchestrator does with it.
- **Does the `done` trigger need both signals** (no unticked task **and** no open/unmerged
  increment PR), or is one sufficient? Two questions over existing artifacts is the proposal;
  justify the choice rather than inheriting it.
