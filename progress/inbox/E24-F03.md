# Intent — Thin the child: umbrella-resolved body with local fallback

## Problem / who
Every child in an umbrella carries a full copy of the harness body — agent prompts, docs,
store contracts, tools, templates, plus the regenerated front-end glue. In the
`~/repos/viernes` cascade that is 26–29 files per child, five times over, all of it
byte-identical and all of it independently able to drift.

For the way the umbrella is actually operated, most of that copy is inert. Claude Code
resolves `.claude/agents/` and `.claude/commands/` from the **session cwd**, so in an
umbrella-rooted session the child's front-end glue is never loaded at all. It exists to be
upgraded, to drift, and to show up in diffs.

Meanwhile the thing that copy is *supposed* to guarantee — that the child works when
entered on its own — is a genuine requirement, not a hypothetical one. The Codex GitHub
App reviews that child's PR. CI runs in that child. A teammate clones only that repo. A
fresh worktree starts there. `/sdd-pr-loop` runs against child PRs. None of those can `cd`
to anyone's umbrella.

So the goal is not "delete the child harness" — it is "stop copying what does not need
copying, keep exactly what standalone entry requires".

## Success outcome
One umbrella has one source of truth for prompt bodies, docs, and role definitions. A child
entered on its own still runs: `init.sh` works, its verification gate works, its PR loop
works. Upgrading the umbrella stops producing N identical diffs.

## Scope / boundaries
In scope:
- Splitting the HARNESS-OWNED set (`harness-install.sh:2199`) into two tiers: what a child
  needs to stand alone, and what can resolve from the umbrella.
- The resolution mechanism: how a child finds its umbrella, and what it does when there
  isn't one.
- The migration path for targets already carrying a full copy.
- An ADR recording the decision (see Constraints).

Out of scope:
- PROJECT-OWNED files. `harness.config.yaml`, `init.project.sh`, `specs/`, `state/`,
  `progress/` stay per-child, always. Per-repo facts — `verification.test_command`, the
  `change_size` budget tuned to that language, the child's own board — are not shareable
  and moving them to the umbrella would break standalone verification, which is the whole
  property being protected.
- Single-repo installs. With no umbrella present the target keeps a complete local body and
  nothing changes for it. This is additive.
- Changing what the umbrella coordinator does with slices, specs, or dispatch
  (`docs/UMBRELLA.md` is correct as written; note that slicing is already umbrella-side —
  shared `.spec`/`.plan` live in the umbrella and per-repo `.tasks`/`.tests` are *emitted
  into* children, so the child body is needed for build/review/PR, not for slicing).

## Chosen options
- **Thin the child; do not empty it.** The child keeps what standalone entry requires —
  `init.sh`, `harness.config.yaml`, the version stamp, the entrypoint block. The prompt
  bodies, `docs/`, `store/`, `tools/`, templates, and front-end glue are the resolvable
  tier.
- **Umbrella wins when present; local copy is the fallback.** A child that can see its
  umbrella reads the umbrella's body. A child that cannot — CI, a lone clone, a PR
  reviewer's checkout — falls back to whatever it has locally and keeps working. The
  fallback is the feature, not a degraded mode.
- **Rejected: "everything at the umbrella, children hold only a pointer, sessions must
  start at root."** It makes the correctness of every session depend on an unenforceable
  human convention, breaks every caller that cannot `cd` upward, and relocates the version
  problem (the umbrella body is still a vendored copy needing an upgrade and a commit)
  rather than removing it. Recorded here because it is the obvious idea and the next person
  will have it too — the ADR should carry the reasoning, not just the verdict.

## Constraints
- **Standalone entry is the invariant.** Any design that makes a child fail when entered
  alone is wrong, regardless of how much duplication it removes. This is the acceptance
  bar.
- **Write an ADR** under `specs/adr/`. How a child locates its umbrella (env var, upward
  search, an explicit path in `harness.config.yaml`) and what it does when it finds none is
  a durable architectural commitment with a real fallback story. `init.sh` already
  hard-fails on a broken environment, so "no umbrella found" must be unambiguously *not*
  broken.
- **Symlinks are a trap worth naming explicitly.** They break on Windows checkouts, they
  get committed by accident, they resolve unpredictably inside git worktrees, and
  `tools/fix-worktree.sh` already carries scar tissue about linking files into fresh
  worktrees (see `docs/CONFIG-LAYERING.md` → caveats). If the design uses them, the ADR
  must say why they survive those cases.
- **Migration must not strand existing targets.** Every currently-installed child has a
  full body. Upgrading into the thin layout must either clean up the now-redundant copy or
  deliberately leave it — but it must be a decision the manifest records, not an accident
  that leaves orphaned files drifting forever. `manifest.txt` is the existing mechanism for
  stating what the installer owns; keep it honest.
- **Depends on F01 and F02.** F01's guard is what will make this rollout observable — if
  thinning goes wrong on a real target, the drift guard is what says so at the top of the
  session instead of three days later.
- Bump `VERSION` (MINOR at least; MAJOR if the installed layout change requires target
  migration — `AGENTS.md` → Versioning is explicit that a breaking layout change is MAJOR).
  Update `docs/UMBRELLA.md`, `docs/INSTALL.md`, and `docs/CONFIG-LAYERING.md`.

## Open questions
- **How does a child locate its umbrella?** Upward filesystem search for
  `umbrella.manifest.yaml` is zero-config but surprising and can escape the intended root;
  an explicit path in the child's `harness.config.yaml` is boring, greppable, and written
  by the cascade that already knows the answer. The second looks right; the ADR should say
  so on the record, having considered the first.
- **What is the exact standalone tier?** `agents/*.md` is the interesting case: a Builder
  spawned by a *child-rooted* session needs its role prompt, so "prompts always resolve from
  the umbrella" may break the standalone invariant. Possibly prompts stay local and only
  `docs/`, `store/`, `tools/`, and templates resolve upward. The Architect should derive
  this from "what does a child-rooted session actually read?" rather than from what looks
  duplicated.
- **Is this one feature or two?** The split-and-resolve mechanism and the migration of
  existing targets may each be a reviewable unit. Per E21, if the honest estimate exceeds
  the change-size budget, say so at spec time and split — do not discover it at PR time.
- Does the thin layout change what `--shared-repo` should track at the umbrella root?
